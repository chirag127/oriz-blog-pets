The myth that cats are aloof and unreadable is mostly a translation problem. Cats are broadcasting information all the time — through the tail, the ears, the eyes, and the whole body. Once you learn the vocabulary, a cat becomes about as mysterious as a slightly dramatic housemate.

## Read the tail first

The tail is the fastest read in the room.

- **Upright, tip curled like a question mark** — a confident, friendly greeting. This is your cat saying hello and inviting interaction.
- **Puffed up (piloerection)** — fear or threat. The cat is trying to look bigger. Give it space; do not reach in.
- **Low or tucked** — anxiety or insecurity.
- **Thrashing or thumping side to side** — agitation or overstimulation. A wagging cat tail is the opposite of a wagging dog tail: it means "back off," not "come here."
- **A gentle twitch at the very tip** — mild interest or focus, often while hunting a toy or watching a bird.

If you learn only one signal, learn the thrash. Most "he bit me for no reason" incidents are a thrashing tail that got ignored during petting.

## Ears are a live mood dial

Cat ears swivel independently and track emotion in real time.

- **Forward and upright** — relaxed, alert, interested.
- **Swivelled sideways or flattened ("airplane ears")** — anxious, irritated, or frightened.
- **Pinned flat back against the head** — a serious warning. The cat feels threatened and may lash out.

Watch the ears together with the tail. Forward ears plus an upright tail is an open, happy cat. Flat ears plus a thrashing tail is a cat asking you, firmly, to stop.

## The slow blink is a compliment

When a relaxed cat looks at you and slowly closes and opens its eyes, that is often called a **cat kiss.** It signals trust — a cat only lowers its guard like that around someone it feels safe with.

You can answer. Look at your cat softly, then do a slow, deliberate blink back. Many cats will return it. It is one of the simplest ways to build a bond, and it works because you are speaking their language instead of expecting them to learn yours.

Avoid hard, unblinking staring. In cat terms a fixed stare reads as a challenge or a threat, which is why cats so reliably walk over to the one guest in the room who dislikes cats.

## Not all purrs mean happiness

Purring usually signals contentment, but not always. Cats also purr when injured, ill, or highly stressed — one theory is that the low-frequency vibration is self-soothing and may even aid healing.

Read the purr in context. A cat kneading your lap with half-closed eyes is a happy purr. A cat purring while hiding, hunched, off its food, or at the vet may be self-comforting through distress. If purring shows up alongside any sign of illness, treat it as a reason to look closer, not a reassurance.

## Other signals worth knowing

- **Head-butting (bunting)** and rubbing against you deposit scent from facial glands. Your cat is marking you as part of its trusted territory. It is a compliment.
- **Kneading ("making biscuits")** is a leftover kittenhood comfort behaviour, tied to nursing. It means the cat feels safe and content.
- **Showing the belly** is usually a sign of trust and relaxation — but it is *not* always an invitation to rub the belly. Many cats will grab your hand if you take the offer. Trust display, not a request.
- **Chattering at birds through the window** is a mix of excitement and frustrated hunting instinct. Harmless, and very funny.

## Behaviour changes are a health signal

Cats hide illness well — an evolutionary hangover from being both predator and prey. That makes sudden behaviour changes one of your best early warning systems.

Take note if a cat suddenly starts hiding, stops using the litter box, grooms far more or far less than usual, changes appetite or thirst, or becomes unusually aggressive or withdrawn. Any abrupt, persistent change in a normally consistent cat is worth a vet conversation. You are not overreacting; you are reading the signals your cat is actually sending.

Learn the tail, the ears, the slow blink, and the baseline of your particular cat, and the "unreadable" animal turns out to have been talking to you the whole time.
